Frequently asked questions

What income do you need to buy a home in Oregon?

With a typical (mid-tier) Oregon home around $504,432, the estimated payment is about $3,808/month, which generally calls for a household income near $163,200 (keeping housing close to 28% of gross income). A starter home (~$355,884) lowers that to roughly $115,157. Your real figure depends on down payment, rate, and local taxes — model it on Home Payment Atlas.

Is Oregon affordable compared with other states?

Oregon ranks 45 of 51 for estimated median-home buying affordability (score 8/100, least affordable), with home values about 41% above the cross-state median. Compare it on the most affordable states ranking.

What does the affordability score mean for Oregon?

The score (8/100) compares the estimated income needed to afford a typical Oregon home with the area median household income. A score above 50 means the median income roughly covers the payment; below 50 means the typical household would need to stretch. Oregon's score of 8 puts it in the "Least affordable" category — meaning a median-income household would need to spend significantly more than the conventional 28% guideline.

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in Oregon?

Estimated monthly ownership costs (~$3,808) run about $2,055 more than the estimated median rent ($1,753), so buyers take on a higher monthly obligation in exchange for building equity. This comparison is directional — actual costs depend on your specific down payment, loan terms, property taxes, insurance, and HOA. Run the full math on Home Payment Atlas.

Where do these Oregon numbers come from?
