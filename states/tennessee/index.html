Frequently asked questions

What income do you need to buy a home in Tennessee?

With a typical (mid-tier) Tennessee home around $329,716, the estimated payment is about $2,489/month, which generally calls for a household income near $106,671 (keeping housing close to 28% of gross income). A starter home (~$186,008) lowers that to roughly $60,171. Your real figure depends on down payment, rate, and local taxes — model it on Home Payment Atlas.

Is Tennessee affordable compared with other states?

Tennessee ranks 30 of 51 for estimated median-home buying affordability (score 19/100, least affordable), with home values about 4% below the cross-state median. Compare it on the most affordable states ranking.

What does the affordability score mean for Tennessee?

The score (19/100) compares the estimated income needed to afford a typical Tennessee home with the area median household income. A score above 50 means the median income roughly covers the payment; below 50 means the typical household would need to stretch. Tennessee's score of 19 puts it in the "Least affordable" category — meaning a median-income household would need to spend significantly more than the conventional 28% guideline.

Is it cheaper to rent or buy in Tennessee?

Estimated monthly ownership costs (~$2,489) run about $1,367 more than the estimated median rent ($1,122), so buyers take on a higher monthly obligation in exchange for building equity. This comparison is directional — actual costs depend on your specific down payment, loan terms, property taxes, insurance, and HOA. Run the full math on Home Payment Atlas.

Where do these Tennessee numbers come from?

Home values are Zillow Research ZHVI (2026-10); rent: U.S. Census ACS median gross rent (ACS 5-year 2023); income is U.S. Census ACS median household income. They are estimates for comparison, not appraisals. Customize the mortgage math on the Tennessee page on Home Payment Atlas.
